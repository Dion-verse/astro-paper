AI tools can be useful for drafting, brainstorming, and organizing information. But before pasting anything into a tool, take a moment to think about what the information contains and who should be allowed to see it.

The safest habit is simple: share only what is necessary, and avoid submitting sensitive information unless you understand the service’s data practices and have permission to use it that way.

## What counts as sensitive information?

Sensitive information can include more than passwords. It may include private messages, financial or medical details, identification numbers, customer records, unreleased business plans, confidential work documents, or information that could identify someone else.

Even a seemingly ordinary document can include names, contact details, or other information that its owner did not intend to share with an outside service.

## Use a “minimum necessary” approach

If you want help rewriting a message, remove names and identifying details first. If you want help summarizing a document, consider whether a short, anonymized excerpt is enough. You might also use fictional examples to test how a tool works.

The less sensitive information you share, the less you have to worry about where it may go.

## Check the tool’s current settings and terms

Different services can have different policies, settings, and controls. Before using a tool for work or personal information, review its current privacy information and any available data-use settings. If the policy is unclear, don’t submit material you would not want handled by that service.

If the information belongs to your employer, client, school, or another person, check the relevant rules and get permission when needed. Your own comfort is not always the only consideration.

## Be cautious with generated answers too

Privacy is not the only issue. An AI response can include errors or make assumptions, so avoid relying on it as the sole source for important decisions. Verify facts using trusted sources, especially for consequential topics.

## A quick checklist

Before submitting content, ask:

- Does this include private or identifying information?
- Do I have permission to share it with this service?
- Can I remove details or use a fictional example instead?
- Have I checked the service’s current data practices and settings?
- Would I be comfortable if this content were stored or processed by the service?

If you cannot answer these questions confidently, pause and find a safer way to complete the task.

Using AI thoughtfully doesn’t mean avoiding it altogether. It means understanding that convenience has trade-offs and building a careful habit before sharing information. A few seconds of checking can prevent an avoidable mistake.
