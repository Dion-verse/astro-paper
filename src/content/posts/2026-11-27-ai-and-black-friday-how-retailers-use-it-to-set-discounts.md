Black Friday is known for deals, but behind some of those prices there may be more than a store manager deciding how much to mark down. Retailers can use artificial intelligence (AI) to analyze information such as sales, inventory, competitor prices, and demand forecasts when planning prices and promotions.

That does not mean every price is set by AI, or that every retailer uses the same system. But a recent Reuters report describes brands using AI pricing tools to make decisions about inventory and discounts. The systems discussed in the report sometimes recommended fewer promotions or smaller discounts when their forecasts suggested that demand could hold.

## What might AI look at?

A retailer may have a large amount of information to consider: which products are selling, how much stock remains, what prices competitors advertise, and how demand changes over time. Software can help analyze those signals and estimate how different pricing choices might affect sales.

That estimate is not a guarantee. A forecast can be wrong, and decisions still depend on a business’s goals, judgment, and the quality of the information it uses. AI is one input into a decision—not a magical way to know exactly what every shopper will do.

## Why might a store offer a smaller discount?

A retailer may decide that a product does not need a deep discount to sell, especially if demand is strong or stock is limited. A smaller promotion can help protect a company’s profit margin. On the other hand, a business might choose a larger discount to clear inventory or attract customers.

Reuters’ reporting describes AI tools being used to help some brands determine when and how much to discount. It also notes that the full effect on consumers is still uncertain. That makes this a developing story rather than proof that all Black Friday bargains will disappear.

## What does this mean for shoppers?

It’s still worth comparing prices and deciding what an item is worth to you. A sale label does not automatically make something a good deal. Check the final price, look at the product’s features, and avoid buying something you do not need simply because a promotion feels urgent.

For costly purchases, compare prices across more than one retailer and check the seller’s return policy. If you’re unsure, taking time to think can be better than rushing because a countdown timer or discount banner suggests you must buy immediately.

## What does it mean for small businesses?

The same general idea—using evidence instead of guessing—can be useful even if a small business does not use sophisticated AI pricing software. A shop owner can look at past sales, current stock, costs, and customer questions before deciding on a promotion.

If considering an AI tool, start by asking what decision it is meant to improve. Check the cost, the information it needs, and how you will judge whether its recommendations are useful. Do not follow a suggested price without considering your costs, customers, and business goals.

## The takeaway

AI is becoming one of the tools some retailers use to think about prices and promotions. For shoppers, the best response is not panic or blind trust: compare offers, know your budget, and make decisions based on the value to you. For businesses, use AI as support for thoughtful decisions—not as a substitute for understanding customers.
